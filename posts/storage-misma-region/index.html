Storage en la misma region es accedido con la IP interna

Si intentas acceder a un storage desde algún servicio de la misma región, en los logs del storage ves la IP interna en lugar de la externa (NAT)

Hoy tuve un caso que me dejó desconcertado.

El cliente tenía la siguiente configuración del datapath: VM –> NVA –> Storage

Y decía que en los logs del Storage, lo que se veía era la IP interna de la VM, en lugar de la public IP del NVA, y que eso solo pasaba con storage que estaba en la misma región que la VM. Que si esa VM intentaba acceder a un Storage en otra región, entonces si se veía la public IP.

Resulta que esto está documentado

Access from VMs in the same region as the storage account use SNAT bypass to eliminate SNAT exhaustion and provide modest performance improvements. Common side effects of this are:

Customers can access storage accounts in configurations that typically can’t access the internet due to lack of a SNAT pool (such as a standard SKU ILB pool member). Customers may see the source IP be either their VNet CA or the container PA in storage account logs. If the container is deployed to a OPAPH cluster, the customer will see the VNet CA as service endpoints are implicitly created by the platform in this scenario. If the container is not deployed to an OPAPH cluster, the customer will see the container PA as the source IP in their storage account logs.

https://supportability.visualstudio.com/AzureNetworking/_wiki/wikis/Wiki/444079/In-region-storage-account-access

Public Documentation Link to public documentation that discusses this: https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/storage/common/storage-network-security?tabs=azure-portal#grant-access-from-an-internet-ip-range

Services deployed in the same region as the storage account use private Azure IP addresses for communication. So, you can’t restrict access to specific Azure services based on their public outbound IP address range.
